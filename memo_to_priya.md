## The short version

I read 18 months of tickets (12,528 in the export, 11,875 once duplicates are removed). The tool gives you a weekly digest of what customers complain about and a leaderboard of Tier 1 agents by tickets closed per week. The most useful finding is somewhere else.

**If you do one thing: cut dead-on-arrival replacements from 1.1% of tickets to 0.8%. That is worth about Rs 1.37 lakh a quarter.**

This is an estimate of what would happen if the rate fell. It is not a promise. I assumed a 30% reduction. Today, about 68 replacements a quarter cost roughly Rs 4.6 lakh (product cost plus Rs 340 shipping each).

## Arjun's question: what does it save?

The tool does not remove contacts by itself. It shows where they come from. Savings only appear when someone acts on what it shows. The three largest opportunities I found:

| Opportunity | Today | Estimated saving per quarter |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer dead-on-arrival replacements | 1.1% of tickets | about Rs 1,37,000 |
| Fewer transfers between teams | 9.1% of tickets | about Rs 31,000 |
| Fewer missed first-response targets | 8.9% of tickets | about Rs 29,000 |

The last two are measured more firmly from timestamps, but they are smaller. Repeat contacts turned out to be small (about 4.9% of tickets), so I did not pick them.

## Two things you asked about

**"I already told your colleague this."** I checked. Tickets that were transferred come back slightly less often (3.8%) than tickets that were not (4.6%). The data does not support this being a widespread problem. The chat team may be noticing a few loud cases.

**The leaderboard.** It is in the tool, labelled "tickets closed per week", and it shows Tier 1 only. It is not a performance score, because voice callbacks take longer than chats. Neha's warranty team appears separately and is not ranked, as her cases take days by design.

## What the weekly digest tells you

Each week: the top complaint types, what changed since last week, which products are involved, and the cost. Delivery, billing, and connectivity are the largest complaint groups overall. Treat those counts as a good guide, not exact figures (see below).

## How far to trust it

I checked the sorting of complaints by hand on 49 tickets. About 7 in 10 were put in the right category, against about 3 in 10 for the current intake bot. That is a small check, so the true figure could be anywhere from roughly 54% to 82%. It struggles most with "Other", damage versus warranty, and product questions. About 6 in 10 tickets were sorted by copying the category of a very similar earlier ticket, not by reading them again. That is faster, but it was less accurate in my check.

## Things I fixed in your data

- 653 tickets appeared twice after the move from Freshdesk. I counted each once.
- Old resolution times were off by 5½ hours (a UTC/IST mix-up), which made many look resolved before they were created.
- A survey score of 0 on old tickets meant "no answer", not a bad rating. I excluded them.
- Two orders received both a refund and a replacement, which policy §5 forbids (about Rs 9,000).
